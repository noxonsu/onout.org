What Is a White Label Crypto Wallet?

A crypto wallet white label solution is pre-built wallet software that you can rebrand and deploy under your own company name. Instead of building a cryptocurrency wallet from scratch, you purchase or license a ready-made product, apply your branding, and launch it as your own. Your end users interact with what looks and feels like a custom-built wallet, while you save months of development time and hundreds of thousands of dollars in engineering costs.

Think of it the same way restaurants use commercial kitchen equipment rather than manufacturing their own ovens. The underlying technology is proven and tested; you focus on the business and customer experience rather than low-level cryptographic engineering.

Why Businesses Choose White Label Wallets

The cryptocurrency market continues to grow, and businesses across industries are exploring ways to offer wallet functionality to their users. Here are the primary reasons a white label approach makes sense:

  • Faster time to market. A custom crypto wallet takes 6 to 12 months to build. White label solutions can be deployed in days or weeks, letting you capture market opportunities while they exist.
  • Lower upfront cost. Custom wallet development starts at $100,000 and can easily exceed $500,000. White label solutions range from under $1,000 to $50,000 depending on the vendor and feature set.
  • Proven security. Reputable white label wallets have been battle-tested by hundreds of deployments. You benefit from security audits and bug fixes that have already been paid for by the vendor and their customer base.
  • Ongoing maintenance. Blockchain protocols evolve constantly. Token standards change, networks upgrade, and new chains launch. A white label vendor handles these updates so you do not need a dedicated blockchain engineering team.
  • Focus on your core business. Whether you run an exchange, a DeFi platform, or a fintech startup, wallet infrastructure is a means to an end. White label lets you focus on what differentiates your business.

The tradeoff is flexibility. A white label wallet gives you less control over the underlying architecture than a fully custom build. For most businesses, however, the speed and cost advantages far outweigh this limitation.

Key Features to Look For

Not all crypto wallet white label products are created equal. When evaluating solutions, pay close attention to these features:

Multi-Currency Support

At minimum, a wallet should support Bitcoin, Ethereum, and major ERC-20 tokens. Better solutions also support BNB Smart Chain (BEP-20), Polygon, and other EVM-compatible networks. The more chains and tokens supported out of the box, the less custom work you need to do later.

Built-In Exchange

Users expect to swap tokens without leaving the wallet interface. Look for integrated exchange functionality, whether through a DEX aggregator like 0x Protocol, or direct integration with Uniswap, PancakeSwap, or similar decentralized exchanges. Without built-in exchange, your wallet is just a balance viewer.

Fiat On-Ramp

The ability to buy crypto with credit cards or bank transfers dramatically improves user onboarding. Solutions that integrate providers like Transak, MoonPay, or Wyre give your users a seamless path from fiat to crypto.

Security Architecture

This is non-negotiable. Understand how the wallet handles private keys. Client-side key generation and storage (non-custodial) is generally preferred because it reduces your liability and regulatory burden. If the wallet is custodial, you need to understand the key management infrastructure, insurance, and compliance requirements.

Customization Options

At a minimum, you should be able to change logos, colors, and domain names. Better solutions let you customize the token list, default networks, fee structures, and UI layout. The goal is for the wallet to look and feel like it was built specifically for your brand.

Deployment Model

Some wallets are SaaS-hosted by the vendor. Others are self-hosted, giving you full control over the infrastructure. Self-hosted solutions typically offer more customization and eliminate ongoing subscription fees, but require you to manage hosting and updates.

Mobile and Web

Consider whether you need a web-based wallet, mobile apps (iOS and Android), or both. Web-based wallets are easier to deploy and update, while native mobile apps offer better performance and push notifications. Some solutions provide both.

Security Considerations

Security is the single most important factor when choosing a crypto wallet white label solution. A vulnerability in your wallet can result in direct financial losses for your users and irreparable damage to your reputation. Here are the key areas to evaluate.

Custodial vs Non-Custodial

In a custodial wallet, your platform holds the private keys on behalf of users. This gives you more control (password resets, account recovery) but also means you are responsible for securing those keys. You become a target for hackers, and in most jurisdictions, holding customer funds triggers financial regulatory requirements.

In a non-custodial wallet, private keys are generated and stored on the user's device. You never have access to their funds. This significantly reduces your liability and regulatory exposure, but it also means users are responsible for their own key backup. If they lose their keys, you cannot help them recover funds.

For most businesses launching a crypto wallet white label product, non-custodial is the safer choice. It eliminates the need for expensive custody infrastructure and reduces your regulatory burden. Solutions like Onout's wallet use this approach, generating keys entirely in the browser.

Private Key Storage

Understand exactly where private keys are stored. Common approaches include:

  • Browser localStorage: Keys are stored encrypted in the browser. Simple and effective for web wallets, but vulnerable if the user's device is compromised.
  • Encrypted file: Keys are exported as an encrypted JSON file that the user stores. More secure but less convenient.
  • Hardware wallet integration: Keys never leave the hardware device. The most secure option for significant amounts.
  • Seed phrase backup: A 12 or 24-word mnemonic that can regenerate all keys. The standard for non-custodial wallets.

Transport Security

All communication between the wallet and blockchain nodes must happen over HTTPS. Verify that the wallet uses encrypted RPC connections and does not leak transaction data to third parties. Check whether the wallet connects to public RPC endpoints (transparent but potentially rate-limited) or requires you to run your own node (more private but more complex).

Regulatory Compliance

Depending on your jurisdiction and business model, offering a crypto wallet may require specific licenses or registrations. Non-custodial wallets generally have fewer regulatory requirements than custodial ones, but this varies by country. Consult with a legal professional familiar with cryptocurrency regulations in your target markets before launching.

Audit and Track Record

Prefer solutions with a proven track record. A wallet with hundreds of deployments and years of operation has been stress-tested in ways that a brand-new product has not. Open-source wallets offer additional transparency because anyone can audit the code. Onout's wallet, for example, is based on the open-source MultiCurrencyWallet project, which has been actively maintained since 2018.
